Five games, one tab, no download

30 August 2026 null 4 min read

Everything we make opens in the browser you already have open, in about a second, on whatever you are holding. That constraint decides almost everything else about how the studio works.

There is one rule at Magestican Studios that decides more than any other: whatever we make has to open in a tab. No download, no launcher, no store, no account, no waiting. You send somebody a link and by the time they have finished reading your message they are playing.

What that rule is actually worth

Most games lose most of their audience before the game starts. Every step between wanting to play and playing costs you people: a store page, an installer, a patcher, a launcher, a login, a tutorial. None of those steps is where the fun is, and every one of them is a place to change your mind.

A link removes all of them at once. It also removes the argument about whether it will run on your machine, and the argument about whether you have space for it.

It is a real constraint, not a slogan. It means we cannot ship a fifty-gigabyte world, and we cannot assume a graphics card. What we can do is have somebody playing in the time it takes an installer to start.

It is why the games look the way they do

Everything is drawn rather than photographed, and generated rather than shipped. The worlds are built from a seed when you open them, which is why they weigh almost nothing and why the same seed always builds the same place. The music is written by the game as it plays instead of streamed. The sound effects are made rather than recorded.

That started as a way to keep the download small and turned into the house style. A hand-drawn look ages far better than a photo-real one, and it means a farmyard kart racer and a team-building shooter can visibly come from the same studio.

Playing with other people should be one link too

The same rule applies to multiplayer. You open a room, you get a link, you send it. Whoever opens it is on the grid — no signup, no friend codes, no lobby to find. Empty seats get filled with bots so a race never waits, and if somebody joins late they are dropped into the race already in progress rather than made to watch.

What we are working on

Farmyshoot is a voxel team shooter built for a room full of colleagues who do not play games. Farmy Kart is the kart racer. 2D Fighter EX is a fighting game. Zelakas in Space is the oldest thing here and still on the site because people still play it. There is a fifth on the bench.

They increasingly share a spine, which is why a fix to how one of them handles a phone tends to show up in all of them shortly afterwards.

The short version

If a game needs a paragraph of instructions before it is fun, it is not finished. If it needs a download before you can find out, it will not be played. Everything we make is built around removing the distance between hearing about it and playing it.

Pick one and see. It costs you a click.
