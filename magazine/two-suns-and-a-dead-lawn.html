Two suns, and a snowfall lit by a dead lawn

29 August 2026 Team Bonding 7 min read

The worst graphics bugs in these games were never reported, because they did not look like bugs. They looked like decisions. Here are five of them, and the working rule that came out of finding them.

A test asserts a value. Only a picture shows a shape, a direction or a colour. That sentence has been earned here about six times, and each time it cost more than the last. These are the five clearest cases.

One: two suns

Farmy Kart got a drawn, smiling sun in the sky. The first version was a flat plane that had to be turned to face the camera every frame — and the preview pages used to check art do not run that logic, so it was edge-on in every screenshot and looked precisely like a sun that had failed to build. It is a sprite now, which the renderer billboards itself and which a caller cannot forget to update.

It is positioned from the key light’s own direction vector, so the drawn sun and the shadows on the ground physically cannot disagree. Doing that meant dropping the key light from 51 degrees of elevation to 30, because at the old height the sun was correct, and existed, and was never once on screen on a 62-degree camera looking along a road.

And then the real one. The sky texture had been painting a sun of its own the whole time — a hard bright core, in a completely different part of the sky.

Two suns in one sky is a defect nobody would ever report as one. It just quietly looks wrong.

The core is gone. The wide haze layers stayed, because a sun with no glow around it is a sticker.

Two: the snow was lit by a dead lawn

Team Bonding is set on snow maps, and the snowfall was darkening between 92 and 100 per cent of the pixels it fell across. Snow that behaves like dirt on the lens.

The sky light in that scene is a hemisphere light: one colour from above, one from below, blended by which way a surface faces. The colour from below was 0x2a4a24 — a dark green, left over from when that map was grass. Its linear luminance is 0.049, about eight per cent of the sky’s.

Every flake in the sky was turned so that the face you see is the face lit from underneath. Every flake in the sky was being lit by a dead lawn.

The same pass found that the flakes’ sideways drift was writing to a local variable that nothing read. Snow had been falling in a perfectly straight vertical line for months, and nobody flagged it, because a broken integration reads as "very subtle" rather than as broken.

Three: the floating square

This one was reported repeatedly, and fixed twice in the wrong place. Both earlier attempts rebuilt the shape of the highlight ring drawn under a player, because the report said a square was floating and the ring was the nearest square-adjacent thing.

The actual cause was that bot bodies were only ever positioned inside the branch that runs during play. In the lobby and during the countdown, all five bots sat at the world origin, sunk a metre into the terrain — so what remained visible was their camera-facing halos and their nameplates, with no character underneath.

countdown   5 remote players   5 of them at the world origin
playing     5 remote players   0 of them at the world origin

A pile of coloured camera-facing quads, in a fixed spot, with nothing under it. Floating, detached-looking, and roughly square. The report was a precise description of the symptom and pointed at entirely the wrong subsystem, twice.

Four: nine sharks on the start line

Farmy Kart’s cliff drops have things underneath them — lava on one circuit, ice on another, and on the third, sharks in a lagoon that lunge at a kart flying overhead and cannot reach it.

The sharks are one instanced mesh, which is how you draw nine of something cheaply. Instances have to be posed at build time. They were not. So all nine sat in a heap at the coordinate origin, which on these circuits is the start line.

It passed every test, because every test asked how many sharks there were and how high they lunge, and the answers were nine and correct.

The clearance is now itself an assertion. A shark’s snout tops out six metres above a waterline fifteen metres below the causeway, so it stops eight metres under a kart merely driving across and twenty-five to thirty metres under one gliding it. That claim spans five numbers in four files and nothing connected them, so it is now checked for every character. Raising the lunge to twelve metres makes it fail — which was confirmed by doing it, and by checking the change had actually reached the disk before believing the red.

Five: the explosion that never ran

A rewritten explosion effect was written, tested, and deployed to the live site on every build for two days, imported by nothing. It executed zero times. The old inline explosion carried on running next to it.

When it was finally wired up, it looked worse than the thing it replaced: smoke cubes specified at half a metre came out at 3.6 metres, and nine layers of soft alpha compounded to nearly opaque black. The blast rendered as a lump of coal with a bit of gold escaping around the edge.

Two separate failures stacked. The published tree now gets crawled the way a browser would — from each page’s script tags through every import — so a module that is shipped but imported by nothing, or imported but not shipped, fails the build instead of the release.

The rule

None of these five was caught by a test, and it is not because the tests were bad. Each defect lived in a property no assertion had a reason to name: which direction a light points, what colour it is, whether two objects that are individually correct are in the same place, whether a correct object is on screen.

A test asserts a value. Only a render shows a shape, a direction or a colour.

So art and lighting work here now ends with a rendered image that somebody looks at, and the image gets taken from the angle a player actually occupies rather than the angle that flatters it. That second half is not a small addition. The thirty-five metre cliff beside every straight in Farmy Kart survived precisely because the preview shots were taken from above, and from above a chasm and a field are both just green.
